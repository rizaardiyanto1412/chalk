# Design a landing page

Use this guide for a page whose visitor needs to understand an offer and decide whether to take it. For a redesign, preserve the supplied brand, real content, and requested scope. Apply this guidance where the user's brief leaves decisions open.

## Decide what the visit should achieve

Choose one conversion goal. Name the audience, the situation that brought them here, and the action that advances their decision. A first-time visitor requesting a consultation needs different evidence from a returning customer buying a familiar item.

Write a short outline before drawing the layout. State the customer's present difficulty, the improvement the offer makes possible, and the reason to believe it. List the questions that could prevent a visitor from continuing. Keep a section only if it answers one of those questions or helps the visitor take the next step.

## Make the opening understandable on its own

The first viewport must identify the offer, explain its value, and present the next action. Use a single headline to carry the main claim. Let supporting copy clarify the audience or mechanism instead of introducing a second pitch.

Prefer a concrete change in the visitor's situation to a list of capabilities. For a delivery service, "Get the evening back from missed deliveries" expresses a human outcome. A short explanation can then connect that outcome to scheduled arrival windows. If the product category is unfamiliar, explain what it is before making an ambitious emotional promise. Never sacrifice clarity to a clever headline.

Name the action precisely, such as "Choose a delivery window" or "Book a site assessment". The label must match what happens next. Keep secondary navigation and alternative actions visually quieter. A visitor should not have to compare several equally prominent buttons to know where to start.

## Support each promise with something observable

Use a product view, a relevant scene, a customer example, or a diagram to explain how the improvement happens. Connect the visual to the headline. A screenshot needs enough surrounding context to show the job it helps someone finish. For a service or lifestyle offer, a scene of the resulting experience may explain more than an isolated interface.

Use the user's assets or inspect the images returned by search_photos before choosing them. Preserve image proportions and crop around the subject. Keep important faces and product details visible on mobile. If text overlaps an image, give the text a stable readable area with a solid panel or a tested scrim. Check the actual crop rather than assuming one overlay works everywhere.

Do not invent endorsements, customer counts, certifications, or performance results. Use supplied evidence. When evidence is unavailable, demonstrate the process or product instead of fabricating social proof. Numerical claims must agree with the details and pass check_numbers.

## Give the page a clear progression

Arrange the material around the visitor's next unanswered question. Establish the offer, show how it helps, provide evidence, address the remaining objection, and repeat the same primary action when it becomes useful. This is a reading sequence, not a required number of sections.

Make adjacent sections do different work. Follow a dense explanation with a demonstration or a short customer example. Vary the amount of text and image area so the page has pauses. Do not repeat identical card grids under different headings.

Choose an alignment for each section and carry it through the heading, paragraph, and action. Independent columns may have their own internal alignment when their relationship remains clear. Keep captions with their images and explanations with their controls. Leave more space between separate subjects than within a related group.

Use a dark or tinted region when it supports the brand or separates an important passage. Darkness alone does not establish trust. Avoid alternating backgrounds merely to make every section look different.

## Make reading and action comfortable

Use a small type scale with obvious differences between the headline, section headings, and supporting text. Set marketing paragraphs around 16 to 18 pixels and never below 14 pixels. Keep paragraph lines roughly 50 to 75 characters long. Center a short statement when the composition calls for it, but use left alignment for longer explanations and lists.

Give the primary action the strongest actionable treatment. Use the action accent consistently. Keep decorative uses limited enough that visitors can still identify the next step immediately. Check WCAG AA contrast for body text, captions, button labels, and text on photographs or colored sections. Do not create emphasis with color alone.

At 390 pixels, preserve the offer and action near the beginning of the reading order. Stack columns deliberately, retain readable type, and provide touch targets of at least 44 pixels. Let a landing page scroll naturally. Do not shrink an entire desktop layout to fit a phone or cover useful content with a fixed action bar.

## Review the rendered result

Build and refine sections in the same frame. Call review_frame and inspect its image, not just the layout measurements. Check the opening at the frame's requested width before polishing the rest of the page. If the user requests both desktop and phone layouts, review each requested frame at its own width. Do not create an extra phone frame just to complete this check.

Confirm that a visitor can identify the offer, expected improvement, reason to trust it, and next action without reading the whole page. Then check the reading order, section density, image crops, text contrast, and action prominence. Remove competing emphasis and repetition. Recheck any repaired section and report unresolved problems honestly.
